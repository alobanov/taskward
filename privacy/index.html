Privacy policy

Last updated: November 4, 2026

Taskward is a to-do list for iPhone and Mac made by Aleksei Lobanov. This policy says what happens to what you write in it. In short: nothing you write reaches us.

What we collect

Nothing of what you write. Taskward has no account, no ads and no tracking. Two things leave your device for someone other than Apple: what is needed to sell Taskward Pro, and usage statistics and crash reports, which you can turn off. Both are described below, and its App Store privacy label says the same.

Where your tasks are kept

Your lists, tasks, notes, tags and settings are kept in a database on your device. If iCloud is on for Taskward, they are also kept in your own iCloud account, in its private database, so your iPhone and Mac stay in step. That database belongs to your Apple Account: we cannot see, read or change it. Apple keeps it under the Apple Privacy Policy.

Your calendars and reminders

If you let it, Taskward reads the events of your calendars to show them next to your day’s tasks. They are read on your device and are never copied anywhere. Reminders you set are scheduled as notifications by your device itself.

You can also make calendar events in Taskward: a new one from the plus, or a task put in your Calendar. On an iPhone this opens the system’s own event editor, which runs apart from Taskward: the event is saved only if you save it there, and Taskward is given no access to your calendars for it. On a Mac, Taskward asks for access to your calendars, saves the event to your default calendar and opens it in the Calendar; Undo takes it out again. An event made so is kept by your calendar like any other and synced by that calendar’s own account, such as iCloud. Taskward keeps no copy of it and sends it nowhere.

Purchases

Taskward Pro is sold through the App Store. Apple handles the payment: we never see your card, your name or your Apple Account.

To know whether Pro is yours and to give it back on a new device, Taskward uses RevenueCat. Each time it starts, the app asks RevenueCat what you hold, under a random identifier made for the installation, not for you, so this happens whether or not you ever buy anything. If you buy, RevenueCat receives the receipt from Apple: which plan, when, at what price, in which country, and whether it renews. No task or anything else you wrote is ever sent. As with any service on the internet, its servers see the network address a request comes from. RevenueCat handles this on our behalf, and it is needed for Pro to work, so it cannot be turned off.

Usage statistics and crash reports

To see what is used and what breaks, Taskward sends usage statistics to Mixpanel and crash reports to Google Firebase Crashlytics. This is on unless you turn it off: Settings, Help improve Taskward. After that nothing more is sent.

Usage statistics say what was done, for example that a task was added or a list opened, a purchase and its plan and price, and how the app is set up: its look, its language, the plan held. With them go the app’s version, your device’s model and system, and the country of your App Store account. Crash reports say what kind of error it was, where in the app, and on what device and system. Both come under a random identifier made for the installation, not for you. No task, note, tag, project name or search is ever sent.

The country and city may be worked out from the network address a request comes from. We ask for this not to happen, but it can, for example when something sent before the app last closed goes out as it starts. Your exact location is never collected.

Where the data goes

RevenueCat is a company in the United States, so purchase records may be processed there. It works under the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses, which bind it to protect them as they would be protected in the EU. Purchase records are needed to give you the Pro you paid for, so they are processed to perform that purchase.

Usage statistics are kept by Mixpanel in the European Union. Crash reports are processed by Google, in the United States among other places, under the same clauses. Both are processed for our legitimate interest in keeping the app working and knowing what to improve, and you can object at any time with the switch in Settings.

If you have chosen to share analytics with Apple, Apple may also send us anonymous crash reports for the app. They contain no tasks or other things you have written.

Deleting your data

Delete a task, a list or everything in the app and it is gone from your device and your iCloud. Deleting the app removes what it kept on the device. To remove what it kept in iCloud, open Settings, tap your name, then iCloud, Manage Storage, Taskward, and delete its data.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, including under the EU and UK GDPR, you have the right to ask what data about you is held, to have it corrected or deleted, to restrict or object to its use, and to receive a copy of it. Write to the address below. As purchase records carry no name or email, we may not be able to tell which of them is yours. The same goes for statistics and crash reports, which carry only the installation’s random identifier. A subscription is stopped in your Apple Account’s settings.

Children

Taskward is not directed at children. It collects nothing that identifies anyone, children included.
